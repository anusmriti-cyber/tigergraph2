Alzheimer’s disease (AD) is a neurodegenerative disease that primarily affects the elderly population and is the leading cause of dementia. Meanwhile, the vascular hypothesis suggests that vascular damage occurs in the early stages of the disease, leading to neurodegeneration and hindered waste clearance, which in turn triggers a series of events including the accumulation of amyloid plaques and Tau protein tangles. Non-coding RNAs (ncRNAs), including long noncoding RNAs (lncRNAs), microRNAs (miRNAs), and circular RNAs (circRNAs), have been found to be involved in the regulation of AD. Furthermore, lncRNAs and circRNAs can act as competitive endogenous RNAs to inhibit miRNAs, and their interactions can form a complex regulatory network. Exosomes, which are extracellular vesicles (EVs), are believed to be able to transfer ncRNAs between cells, thus playing a regulatory role in the brain by crossing the blood-brain barrier (BBB). Exosomes are part of the intercellular carrier system; therefore, utilizing exosomes to deliver drugs to recipient cells might not activate the immune system, making it a potential strategy to treat central nervous system diseases. In this review, we review that AD is a multifactorial neurological disease and that ncRNAs can regulate its multiple pathogenic mechanisms to improve our understanding of the etiology of AD and to simultaneously regulate multiple pathogenic mechanisms of AD through the binding of ncRNAs to exosomes to improve the treatment of AD. While significant breakthroughs have been made in understanding the pathogenesis and progression of AD, diagnosis remains challenging, and effective treatment is a major unresolved issue. According to the traditional amyloid cascade hypothesis, the deposition of β-amyloid (Aβ) plays a crucial role in the pathogenesis of AD and is a typical characteristic of the disease (Karran et al. 2011 ). Aβ is believed to be the initiating event that triggers AD and leads to a series of downstream events, such as Tau protein hyperphosphorylation, oxidative stress, inflammation, and synaptic dysfunction (Chen et al. 2017 ; Karran et al. 2011 ). However, the vascular hypothesis suggests that vascular damage exists in the early development of AD, even before classical biomarker changes occur (Bell and Zlokovic 2009 ), leading to neurodegeneration and hinderance of the clearance of metabolic waste products (Eisenmenger et al. 2023 ). The “two-hit” vascular hypothesis proposes that the first hit is not related to Aß and includes microvascular damage such as disruption of the blood-brain barrier (BBB) and reduction of cerebral blood flow (CBF), resulting in vascular- mediated neuronal dysfunction, hypoxic lesions, and impaired clearance of hazardous waste metabolites (e.g., Aβ). Subsequent amyloid toxicity exacerbates microvascular damage through a positive feedback loop, resulting in a second hit (Eisenmenger et al. 2023 ). At the same time, it has been shown that in the complex pathogenesis of AD there is an interaction between various pathological factors (Nasb et al. 2024 ). Initially, our understanding of AD was limited. However, with advances in technology and continuous research, it has been determined that certain non-coding RNAs (ncRNAs), including long non-coding RNAs (lncRNAs), microRNAs (miRNAs), and circular RNAs (circRNAs), directly or indirectly participate in the regulation, and possibly, the pathophysiology of AD (Lauretti et al. 2021 ; Martinez and Peplow 2019 ). Furthermore, studies have also found that lncRNAs (Liu et al. 2022a , b ) and circRNAs (Hansen et al. 2013 ; Zang et al. 2020 ) can function as competing endogenous RNAs for miRNAs, exerting a “sponge” function to inhibit the activity of miRNAs. By understanding the interactions between these molecules, it is possible to construct lncRNA-miRNA-mRNA networks (Li et al. 2024 ; Ma et al. 2020a , b ) and circRNA-miRNA-mRNA networks (Chen et al. 2021a , b , c , d ; Li et al. 2024 ), thus providing a more comprehensive understanding of the regulatory mechanisms underlying the development of AD. Extracellular vesicles (EVs) are categorized into exosomes (30–100 nm), microvesicles (50–1000 nm), and apoptotic vesicles (50–5000 nm) based on their origin, size, and mode of release (Beeraka et al. 2020 ). Meanwhile, evs, specifically exosomes, have emerged as crucial cell-to-cell communication vehicles for the transfer of ncRNAs between neurons and body fluids (Wang et al. 2022 ). Recent studies have shown that exosomes can carry ncRNAs and cross the blood-brain barrier, allowing them to exert regulatory effects in the brain and modulate its physiological functions (Bhatt et al. 2021 ; Yang et al. 2021 ). Additionally, as part of the body’s intercellular cargo system, utilizing exosomes as carriers to deliver drugs to recipient cells might not activate the immune system (Iranifar et al. 2019 ). Therefore, these inherent advantages make exosomes a potential safe and effective communication carrier for the treatment of central nervous system diseases. Through intranasal administration, drugs are absorbed into the olfactory bulb through the olfactory epithelium inside the nasal cavity, and then directly enter the brain through the olfactory nerve (Ma et al. 2020a , b ). In this process, specific surface ligands may allow recipient cells to efficiently and selectively uptake these vesicles (Schneider and Simons 2013 ). This drug delivery method can bypass the blood-brain barrier and be delivered to the affected area, thereby regulating the production and clearance of Aβ, tau phosphorylation, inflammatory response, neural protection, neural regeneration, oxidative stress, and microglia. In this review, the purpose of our research was to use PubMed and Wanfang database to conduct a literature review, search for keywords such as “Alzheimer’s disease, AD, exosome, circRNA, lncRNA, miRNA, ceRNA”, sort out and classify related studies, and propose a strategy for the treatment of AD by regulating the pathogenic mechanism of ncRNAs and exosomes. Important pathophysiologic processes in AD and therapeutic effects of related drugs AD is the most common neurodegenerative disease and is also considered a multifactorial disease, but its exact pathogenic mechanism remains unclear (Lauretti et al. 2021 ). Continuous research has found that hypotheses about AD, such as amyloid protein cascades, Tau hyperphosphorylation, cholinergic deficiency, neuroinflammation, oxidative stress, mitochondrial dysfunction, and vascular dysfunction, are not mutually exclusive. They all play a role in the occurrence and development of AD and can also promote each other (Nasb et al. 2024 ). Additionally, the polarization of microglial cells is also a pathogenic mechanism for AD. Their dysfunction can lead to insufficient accumulation and clearance of Aβ (Grubman et al. 2021 ), drive Tau phosphorylation/neuron aggregation, and lead to the spread of Tau pathology in the brain (Maphis et al. 2015 ). Moreover, microglial cells are not only the primary resident innate immune cells in the central nervous system, participating in immune responses (Yousefizadeh et al. 2022 ), but also play a crucial role in maintaining the stability of neuronal synapses (Sominsky et al. 2018 ) and the plasticity of synapses (Milinkeviciute et al. 2019 ). Acetylcholinesterase inhibitors (AChE) and AD Tacrine Tacrine is an acridine-derived cholinergic that acts as a central inhibitor of acetylcholinesterase, increasing acetylcholine levels in various brain regions (Wagstaff and McTavish 1994 ). Its inhibitory effect on pseudocholinesterase is more potent than on acetylcholinesterase itself. In September 1993, the drug was approved by the US Food and Drug Administration (FDA). Its main advantages are the ability to be administered orally and intravenously, and its capacity to cross the BBB (Pardo-Moreno et al. 2022 ). However, as its use has become more widespread, studies have found that Tacrine can alleviate symptoms in patients with mild to moderate dementia but does not alter the underlying process of neurodegenerative change (Jarrott 2017 ). Additionally, it can cause adverse reactions such as nausea, vomiting, loss of appetite, diarrhea, and clumsiness, as well as hepatocellular toxicity, which has been reported as a consequence of its administration (Lou et al. 2015 ). Donepezil Donepezil is a piperidine-derived cholinergic drug that acts as a central, reversible, and non-competitive inhibitor of acetylcholinesterase, the enzyme responsible for the hydrolysis of acetylcholine. It is involved in the pathogenesis of AD at the molecular and cellular levels, inhibiting various aspects of glutamate-induced excitotoxicity, reducing the early expression of inflammatory cytokines, inducing the neuroprotective isoform of AChE, and mitigating the effects induced by oxidative stress (Jacobson and Sabbagh 2008 ). It was approved in 1996 for the treatment and management of mild to moderate AD. However, research has found that it does not block the progression of AD (Pardo-Moreno et al. 2022 ). Galantamine Galantamine is a selective, competitive, and reversible acetylcholinesterase inhibitor. In addition, it is not only a selective tertiary isoquinoline alkaloid but also stimulates the intrinsic action of acetylcholine on nicotinic receptors (Pardo-Moreno et al. 2022 ). It acts at the level of the central nervous system while having minimal activity in the peripheral system (Hanafy et al. 2016 ; Misra et al. 2016 ). The drug was approved by the FDA in 2001. Studies have found that while the drug can effectively control behavioral symptoms, improve performance in activities of daily living, and cognitive function, making it a drug of choice for treating AD, it also has a number of adverse reactions, such as seizures, severe nausea, stomach cramps, vomiting, irregular breathing, confusion, muscle weakness, and lacrimation (Haake et al. 2020 ). Rivastigmine Rivastigmine is a pseudo-irreversible inhibitor of acetylcholinesterase and butyrylcholinesterase, acting by binding to the anionic and esteratic sites of acetylcholinesterase (Desai and Grossberg 2005 ). The drug was approved by the FDA in 2000 for the treatment of mild to moderate AD. As research has progressed, adverse reactions such as stomach pain, weight loss, diarrhea, loss of appetite, nausea, and vomiting have been observed in patients. Additionally, the overdose of this drug can cause symptoms such as irregular breathing (fast or slow), chest pain, slow or irregular heartbeat (Pardo-Moreno et al. 2022 ). N-Methyl D-Aspartate (NMDA) antagonists and AD Memantine Memantine is a non-competitive, moderate-affinity, voltage-dependent NMDA receptor antagonist. It blocks the action of pathologically elevated glutamate levels, which can lead to neuronal dysfunction. Glutamate-mediated neurotransmission, particularly the dysfunction of NMDA receptors, contributes to the expression and progression of symptoms in AD towards neurodegenerative dementia (Pardo-Moreno et al. 2022 ). The drug was approved by the FDA in 2003 for the treatment of severe AD. However, it has a number of adverse reactions, including dizziness, headache, confusion, diarrhea and constipation, hypertension, weight gain, hallucinations, disorientation, aggressive behavior, vomiting, abdominal pain, and urinary incontinence (Rossom et al. 2004 ). Aducanumab and AD Aducanumab is a monoclonal antibody targeting soluble Aβ protofibrils and oligomers, with its application aimed at clearing Aβ plaques (Liu and Howard 2021 ). The drug was recently approved by the FDA in April 2021 because it is believed to act on senile plaques (SP) and slow the progression of AD. Clinical trials have confirmed that while it can significantly reduce amyloid deposits, it may cause adverse reactions such as edema or hemosiderin deposition (Pardo-Moreno et al. 2022 ). Based on the pathogenic mechanism, the developed cholinesterase inhibitors (Tacrine, Donepezil, Galantamine, Rivastigmine), N-Methyl D-Aspartate (NMDA) Antagonists (Memantine), and Aβ monoclonal antibody (Aducanumab) have only moderate clinical benefits and are not effective in treating AD, or they have significant side effects. Therefore, discovering new, more effective, and less side-effect-prone AD treatment methods is urgent. As research deepens, the potential relationship between ncRNAs and the pathophysiology of AD has been discovered. For instance, abnormal expression of ncRNAs and their downstream mRNA has been observed in the central nervous system, cerebrospinal fluid (CSF), and serum of patients with AD, indicating their involvement in the pathogenesis of the disease (Martinez and Peplow 2019 ; Wang et al. 2019a , b ). Although their mechanism is not yet fully elucidated, studies suggest that these highly conserved ncRNAs are important regulators of gene expression, Aβ production, tau phosphorylation, inflammation, synaptic plasticity, and neuronal survival, all of which are considered core characteristics of AD pathogenesis (Lauretti et al. 2021 ). Therefore, regulating the expression of ncRNAs may become an effective method for treating AD. Reduction in Aβ aggregation Plaques formed by amyloid aggregation, which are characterized by the accumulation of misfolded proteins in the brain, have consistently been identified as the principal pathological hallmark of AD (Gao et al. 2024 ). It is widely acknowledged that targeting the reduction of Aβ aggregation is important in the pursuit of effective AD treatments. Numerous scientific investigations have shown that abnormalities in Aβ production and metabolism are associated with the disease (Olufunmilayo et al. 2023 ), so measures aimed at lowering the production of Aβ proteins and augmenting their clearance mechanisms can effectively hinder the aggregation of Aβ in the brain. Interestingly, the orchestration of these regulatory processes is intricately governed by various ncRNAs (Lauretti et al. 2021 ). These RNA molecules either directly or indirectly influence the production and clearance of Aβ, thereby playing a crucial role in the modulation of AD progression and offering potential avenues for therapeutic interventions. Reduction in Aβ production CircRNAs are more abundant in neuronal tissues, typically originating from neuron-specific genes involved in synaptic function (Sekar and Liang 2019 ). CircRNAs have been shown to act as sponges for miRNAs, thereby regulating the functions of these miRNAs (D’Anca et al. 2022 ). They not only reduce oxidative stress and neuroinflammation but also impact the formation and metabolism of Aβ (Vakili et al. 2022 ). The discovery of circRNAs has opened up a new window into the neurodegenerative mechanisms underlying AD and related dementias. High-expressed circHDAC9 exerts a protective effect by regulating the miR-138/Silent information regulator 1 (sirtuin1/ SIRT1) axis, which indirectly increases SIRT1 levels by decreasing miR-138 expression, thereby reducing Aβ production in AD cell models (Lu et al. 2019 ). It was found that circKIAA1586 might act as a sponge for three AD-associated miRNAs (miR-29b, miR-101 and miR-15a), the expression levels of which were reduced in patients with AD (Zhang et al. 2019a , b ). Studies have shown that miR-15a can regulate AD progression by targeting the Amyloid precursor protein (APP) and β-site amyloid precursor protein cleaving enzyme 1 (BACE1) (Hébert et al. 2008 ). miR-29b was reduced in AD patients with BACE1 and subsequent Aβ overexpression (Pereira et al. 2016 ), and inhibition of miR-101 increased APP levels and affected Aβ accumulation (Vilardo et al. 2010 ). Therefore, indirect regulation of downstream targets through direct regulation of circRNAs to further regulate and reduce Aβ production might be a promising approach to treat AD. The human genome contains many lncRNAs, with a majority being expressed in the brain (Derrien et al. 2012 ). Research has shown that levels of certain lncRNAs are significantly changed in the brains of patients with AD (Cao et al. 2019 ; Zhou and Xu 2015 ). Studies have revealed the role of lncRNAs in AD, particularly in Aβ metabolism, showing that they can affect Aβ production and clearance through various mechanisms (Cortini et al. 2019 ). Some lncRNAs such as highly expressed lncBACE1-AS (Faghihi et al. 2008 ; Hao et al. 2022 ), lncMAGI2-AS3 (Hao et al. 2022 ; Zhang and Wang 2021 ; Zhang et al. 2021 ), lncBC200 (Hao et al. 2022 ; Li et al. 2018 ), lncNEAT1 (Hao et al. 2022 ; Zhao et al. 2019 ), lncNDM29 (Hao et al. 2022 ; Massone et al. 2012 ), lncXIST (Hao et al. 2022 ; Yue et al. 2020 ) and lncBDNF-AS (Ding et al. 2022 ; Hao et al. 2022 ) indirectly increase mRNA expression by directly interacting with downstream target miRNAs to lower their expression levels, promoting Aβ generation. On the other hand, low expression of lncCYP3A43-2 (Wuli et al. 2022 ) leads to increased miRNA expression and indirectly decreases mRNA expression, exerting an inhibitory role in Aβ generation (Table 1 ) (Fig. 1 ). These findings suggest that ncRNAs might play an important role in the pathogenesis and progression of AD, and have the potential to be therapeutic targets and diagnostic biomarkers for the disease. Enhancement of Aβ clearance There are multiple pathways to accomplish the removal of Aβ: (I) Aβ can be cleared through lymphatic drainage (Van Gool et al. 2019 ); (II) the autophagy-lysosome pathway can also contribute to the clearance of Aβ (Liu et al. 2017 ; Mandrekar et al. 2009 ); (III) Aβ can enter the circulation by crossing the blood-brain barrier (Storck et al. 2018 ; Van Gool et al. 2019 ); and (IV) the degradation of Aβ can also occur through the action of extracellular proteases (Yoon and Jo 2012 ). Autophagy and protease degradation are currently known to be regulated by ncRNAs. Enhanced autophagy to reduce Aβ aggregation Autophagy is partly responsible for the elimination of Aβ aggregates and neuronal Tau proteins (Heckmann et al. 2020 ; Pandit et al. 2019 ; Wani et al. 2019 ). Autophagy dysfunction leads to Aβ and Tau accumulation and promotes AD progression (Fedeli et al. 2019 ; Ulland et al. 2017 ). It was found that circ_017963 might act as a sponge for miR-7033-3p and participate in biological processes such as autophagosome assembly, extracellular secretion, and synaptic vesicle recycling (Zhang et al. 2020 ). CircNF1-419 might enhance autophagy by interacting with adapter protein 2B1 (AP2B1) and Dynamin-1 to induce astrocyte autophagy and thereby delay AD progression (Diling et al. 2019 ). Indeed, overexpression of circNF1-419 in senescent SAMP8 mice enhanced autophagy, reduced Aβ1–42 aggregation, and affected the expression levels of senescence-associated regulators p16, p21, and p35/25 (Zhang et al. 2020 ). Thus, it is possible to increase Aβ clearance by adjusting the expression of circRNAs to increase autophagy. Enhanced ubiquitination and degradation of Phosphatase and tensin homolog induced kinase 1 (PINK1) by upregulated lncNEAT1 inhibits autophagy signaling and leads to amyloid accumulation (Huang et al. 2020 ). LncLRP1 mediates phagocytosis and degradation of Aβ by astrocytes, neurons, and microglia, which is important in Aβ clearance (Kanekiyo et al. 2013 ; Liu et al. 2017 ) (Fig. 1 ). Silencing of miR-140 is associated with improved cellular autophagy (Liang et al. 2021a , b ) Experiments showed that by targeting the autophagy receptors Sequestosome 1 (Sqstm1) and optineurin, miR-331-3p and miR-9-5p regulated the elimination of Aβ. Inhibition of miR-331-3p and miR-9-5p promoted the autophagic clearance of Aβ, and the learning and memory abilities of AD mice were improved (Chen et al. 2021a , b , c , d ). An miR-485-3p antisense oligonucleotide enhanced Aβ clearance via CD36-mediated Aβ phagocytosis (Koh et al. 2022 ). In addition, studies have shown that administration of miR-485-3p antisense oligonucleotide reduces apoptosis, decreases levels of truncated tau and decreases secretion of pro-inflammatory cytokines, including IL-1β and TNF-α, and ultimately alleviates cognitive impairment (Koh et al. 2022 ) (Table 1 ). Enhanced protease degradation to reduce Aβ aggregation Aβ is metabolized from the precursor protein by β-secretase and γ-secretase (Liu et al. 2024 ). One method to reduce Aβ levels in the AD brain is to inhibit Aβ production through pharmacology, while another method is Aβ clearance (Liu et al. 2024 ). Brain clearance of Aβ requires enzymatic degradation, absorption into the blood, and drainage along the perivascular lymphatic pathway (Diem et al. 2016 ). Extracellular Aβ is degraded by insulin-degrading enzyme and neprilysin, while intracellular Aβ is membrane-associated and may therefore be degraded by lysosomes (de Dios et al. 2019 ). CiRS-7 can play a neuroprotective role in AD by regulating the miR-7/Ubiquitin conjugating enzyme 2 A (UBE2A) axis (Vakili et al. 2022 ). Deletion of ciRS-7 resulted in a reduced sponging effect on miR-7, which might lead to apparent miR-7 overexpression, thus reducing the expression of the AD-associated miR-7 target UBE2A (Vakili et al. 2022 ). Amyloid deposition and age spot formation are due to UBE2A downregulation in the hippocampus of patients with AD (Guglielmotto et al. 2012 ). Furthermore, it has been observed that in the context of AD, the upregulation of lncXIST leads to a decrease in the expression of Neprilysin (NEP), which in turn leads to a decrease in the enzymatic clearance of Aβ from cells (Yan et al. 2022 ). This impaired clearance mechanism ultimately contributes to the aggregation of Aβ, a hallmark feature of AD pathology (Table 1 ) (Fig. 1 ). Therefore, by increasing the enzymatic clearance of Aβ through the regulation of ncRNAs, it is possible to reduce Aβ aggregation and potentially slow the progression of AD. Fig. 1 Pathologic modulation of AD by ncRNAs Reduction in hyperphosphorylated Tau aggregation and prevention of fibrous tangles Research has found that apart from Aβ, hyperphosphorylated Tau also plays a crucial role in AD development (Kang et al. 2014 ). However, a large body of evidence suggests that there are independent and synergistic mechanisms between Aβ and Tau (Karran and De Strooper 2022 ; Nisbet et al. 2015 ; Zhang et al. 2022 ). The formation of insoluble fibrillary tangles occurs when hyperphosphorylated Tau aggregates, leading to the disruption of synaptic transmission (Chen et al. 2021a , b , c , d ). Further studies demonstrated that the production and clearance of hyperphosphorylated Tau are directly or indirectly regulated by lncRNAs (Hao et al. 2022 ; Xu et al. 2020 ), circRNAs (Li et al. 2020 ), miRNAs (El Fatimy et al. 2018 ; Hou et al. 2020 ), and mRNAs. Reduced production of hyperphosphorylated Tau It has been hypothesized that circPCCA might exert its regulatory effect through the miR-138-5p/Retinoic acid receptor alpha (RARA)/Glycogen synthase kinase 3 Beta (GSK-3β) axis. CircPCCA appears to be a sponge for miR-138-5p (Li et al. 2020 ), whose downstream target is the RARA, and high expression of RARA drastically reduces the expression of GSK-3β, ultimately decreasing tau phosphorylation (Wang et al. 2015 ). Low expression lncNEAT1 leads to a decrease in the levels of Tau. This reduction is mediated through a pathway involving the interaction between miR-146a-5p and miR-34a-5p, and the Receptor tyrosine kinase-like orphan receptor 1 (ROR1) (Hao et al. 2022 ). The downregulation of lncSOX21-AS1 weakens the hyperphosphorylation of Tau. This effect is achieved by acting as a sponge for miR-107, thereby preventing its interaction with Tau and subsequent hyperphosphorylation (Xu et al. 2020 ) (Table 1 ) (Fig. 1 ). With further in-depth study of AD, increasing numbers of miRNAs were found to be involved in the regulation of Tau phosphorylation. Highly-expressed miR-23b-3p (Jiang et al. 2022a , b ), miR-106b (Liu et al. 2016 ), and miR-137 (Jiang et al. 2018 ) will decrease the expression of downstream mRNA, thereby inhibiting Tau phosphorylation.Highly expressed miR-98 (Hu et al. 2013 ), miR-125b (Ma et al. 2022 ), miR-138 (Wang et al. 2015 ), and miR-922 (Zhao et al. 2014 ) promote Tau phosphorylation by downregulating downstream mRNA. The low-expressed miR-195 (Sun et al. 2015 ) and miR-219-5p (Li et al. 2019a , b ) promote Tau phosphorylation by decreasing and increasing the expression of downstream target mRNA, respectively (Table 1 ). This might be a completely new angle to study the treatment of AD, and the use of exosome technology to regulate the levels of miRNAs to achieve the regulation of Tau phosphorylation. Therefore, the reduction of Tau hyperphosphorylation achieved by modulating the regulation of ncRNAs and mRNA expression through exosome technology is expected to be a novel approach in AD therapy. Understanding the mechanisms of neuroinflammation is helpful to develop methods to slow down the progression of AD and new treatment strategies. NcRNAs play an important role in regulating neuroinflammatory signaling (Ma et al. 2020a , b ). As shown by the expression of circ-Epc1 (Liu et al. 2022a , b ), circ-AXL (Li et al. 2022a , b , c ), circNF1-419 (Diling et al. 2019 ), and cIRS-7 (Cai et al. 2020 ), the inhibition of inflammatory response is achieved through the indirect regulation of mRNA or protein expression by acting on downstream miRNA. Similarly, the low expression of circ_0003611 (Pan et al. 2022 ) also plays a suppressive role in the inflammatory response by acting on miRNA. On the other hand, the high expression of circ_0000950 (Yang et al. 2019 ) promotes the inflammatory response by acting on downstream mRNA. Additionally, the high expression of lncMALAT1 (Ma et al. 2019 ) indirectly promotes the inflammatory response by acting on downstream miRNA, while the low expression of lncANRIL (Zhou et al. 2020 ) and the high expression of lncMEG3 (Yi et al. 2019 ) indirectly inhibit the inflammatory response by acting on downstream targets (Fig. 1 ). They are highly prevalent in many diseases and plays a key role in controlling inflammation (Zingale et al. 2021 ). Some ncRNAs might have specific roles in certain diseases, while others interact with multiple ncRNAs, leading to cumulative effects on neuroinflammation during disease. Understanding the specific mechanisms of ncRNAs in these diseases, including AD, can contribute to the development of diagnostic and therapeutic applications. Further research showed that the occurrence and development of inflammation in patients with AD were also regulated by miRNAs. Highly expressed miR-29c-3p (Sha et al. 2021 ), and miR-338-5p (Qian et al. 2019 ), and lowly expressed miR-27a (Hu et al. 2021 ), miR-128 (Liu et al. 2019 ), and miR-140-5p (Khodabakhsh et al. 2023 ), act on downstream targets and pathways to exert inhibitory effects on inflammatory responses (Table 2 ). This might represent a new approach to treat AD, where the regulation of inflammation is achieved by the regulation of miRNAs through exosome technology. Reducing oxidative damage and enhancing neuroprotection Mutated APP and soluble Aβ might enter mitochondria, thereby inducing free radicals to further impair mitochondrial metabolism (Manczak et al. 2006 ). The loss of balance between Reactive oxygen species (ROS) and antioxidant defenses is characteristic of oxidative stress, which is further followed by a series of consequences, such as oxidation of proteins, sugars, and DNA, lipid peroxidation, and altered glucose metabolism (Gella and Durany 2009 ). A contributing factor to the progression of aging and AD is oxidative stress; therefore, reducing oxidative damage can help slow the progression of AD. In addition, studies have shown that miRNAs, circRNAs, and lncRNAs have all been implicated in oxidative stress and many other important pathophysiological processes in the onset and progression of AD (Olufunmilayo and Holsinger 2023 ). Overexpression of circPRKCI increases E2F transcription factor 7 (E2F7) expression levels and reduces oxidative stress through regulation of the miR-545/E2F7 axis and the miR-589/E2F7 axis, thereby reducing cell death (Cheng et al. 2019 ). In addition, E2F7 is a relatively new transcription factor that can form a heterodimer with E2F1 and recruit corepressor C-terminal-binding protein (CtBP) to regulate the cell cycle by inhibiting the expression of G1-S genes in late S phase (Cheng et al. 2019 ). Low-expressing circ_0003611 reduce oxidative stress by targeting the miR-383-5p/Kinesin Family Member 1B (KIF1B) axis. Circ_0003611 acts as a miR-383-5p sponge, and miR-383-5p directly targets KIF1B, and the low-expressing circ_0003611 weakens the sponge effect of miR-383-5p, resulting in an up-regulation of miR-383-5p and a decrease in KIF1B expression to alleviate neuronal damage caused by Aβ-triggered oxidative stress (Li et al. 2022a , b , c ). Low expression of circLPAR1 protects cells from oxidative stress via the growth differentiation factor 15 (GDF-15)/SIRT1/Nuclear factor erythroid 2-related factor 2 (Nrf2)/ HO-1 axis. Knockdown of circLPAR1 and overexpression of GDF-15 protected cells from oxidative stress and neuronal apoptosis, and SIRT1 knockdown reversed GDF-15 overexpression-induced inhibition of Aβ-induced neuronal injury and inhibition of the Nrf-2/HO-1 pathway (Xiong et al. 2023 ). Low expression of lncNKILA can inhibit neuronal damage and oxidative stress through the Forkhead box protein A1 (FOXA1)/Tumor necrosis factor alpha-induced protein 1 (TNFAIP1) axis. Knockdown of lncNKILA reduces the expression of FOXA1, thereby alleviating oxidative stress-induced apoptosis. Overexpression of TNFAIP1 can reverse this phenomenon. Additionally, the transcription process of TNFAIP1 is controlled by FOXA1, which targets the promoter of TNFAIP1 (Zhou et al. 2023 ). Low expression of lncH19 reduces oxidative stress through the miR-129/High mobility group box 1 (HMGB1) axis. LncH19 can specifically bind to miR-129, while miR-129 specifically inhibits HMGB1 expression. Low expression of HMGB1 reduces mitochondrial membrane potential dysfunction and ROS production, increases mitochondrial membrane potential (MMP), reduces oxidative stress, enhances cell viability, and decreases apoptosis (Zhang et al. 2021 ). Overexpression of lncMEG3 not only inhibited hippocampal neuronal apoptosis, reduced Aβ expression, and attenuated inflammatory damage, but also reduced oxidative stress through inhibition of the PI3K/Akt signaling pathway (Yi et al. 2019 ). Overexpression of lncWT1-AS is regulated through the miR-375/SIX homeobox 4 (SIX4) axis, indirectly enabling increased SIX4 expression and reducing oxidative stress damage and apoptosis. Overexpression of WT1-AS can inhibit the expression of WT1, and WT1 can target miR-375 to promote its expression, so overexpression of WT1-AS can indirectly reduce miR-375. miR-375 binds to SIX4, and low expression of miR-375 can promote the expression of SIX4 (Wang et al. 2020 ) (Fig. 1 ). Overexpression of miR-204-3p acts on the downstream target NADPH oxidase 4 (Nox4) to inhibit its expression, reduce oxidative stress, and attenuate memory deficits (Tao et al. 2021 ). Low expression of miR-592 upregulates KIAA0319 by activating the Kelch like ECH associated protein 1 (Keap1)/ Nrf2/Antioxidant response element (ARE) signaling pathway, which reduced oxidative damage in an AD rat model (Wu et al. 2020 ) (Table 2 ). Thus increased neuroprotection through modulation of oxidative stress through the expression of ncRNAs may become a new therapy for AD. Improving vascular dysfunction and delaying disease progression Vascular dysfunction plays a key role in the pathogenesis of AD (Sweeney et al. 2019 ; Tsartsalis et al. 2024 ; Zhao et al. 2021 ), which involves the loss of structural or functional integrity of the vasculature (Ali et al. 2022 ). Studies have shown that many ncRNAs also play an important role in the BBB modulation of CNS diseases (Sun et al. 2022 ), and that disruption of the BBB exacerbates the accumulation of Aβ, thereby accelerating the progression of AD (Matsuo and Nshihara 2024 ; Sun et al. 2022 ). Data suggest that cerebrovascular miRNAs associated with endothelial function, vascular permeability, angiogenesis, inflammation, and Aβ/tau metabolism can track the early development of AD (Chum et al. 2022 ). LncMIAT is widely expressed in endothelial cells, glial cells, and neurons, and is a regulator of vascular dysfunction, including retinal angiogenesis, corneal angiogenesis, and vascular permeability (Jiang et al. 2016 ). Furthermore, lncMIAT plays a critical role in diabetesinduced microvascular dysfunction (Yan et al. 2015 ). In AD, a neurovascular disease of the central nervous system, lncMIAT knockout led to cerebral microvascular degeneration, progressive neuronal loss, and neurodegeneration, as well as behavioral deficits, suggesting that lncMIAT plays a crucial role in the pathogenesis of neurovascular dysfunction (Jiang et al. 2016 ). In addition, it was found that lncMIAT is involved in the maintenance of normal microvascular and neural function, and regulates neural and vascular cell function by regulating the miR-150-5p/vascular endothelial growth factor (VEGF) axis (Jiang et al. 2016 ). Low expression of LINC006622 decreases BBB permeability by increasing ETS-domain protein 4 (ELK4) expression and thereby decreasing BBB permeability (Liu et al. 2020a , b ). Overexpression of miR-124 alleviates learning and memory deficits in APP/PS1 mice, and its reduced expression by acting on C1q-like protein 3 (C1ql3) rescues disruption of the BBB, promotes angiogenesis, and reduces Aβ deposition (Li et al. 2019a , b ) (Table 2 ). Thus, ncRNAs might be a promising therapeutic target for the treatment of neurovascular dysfunction related diseases. Enhancing neuroplasticity and neurogenesis In recent years, neuroplasticity has become an important hotspot in brain injury research (Chang et al. 2024 ). Synaptic plasticity and neurogenesis are the main manifestations of neuroplasticity in the adult central nervous system (Chipika et al. 2022 ; Colom-Cadena et al. 2020 ). It is well known that Hippocampal synaptic plasticity and neurogenesis deficits (HIPP) have an important impact on cognitive impairment (Berger et al. 2020 ; Mercerón-Martínez et al. 2021 ). With the deepening of research, it was found that neuroplasticity and neurogenesis are also regulated by ncRNAs (Butler et al. 2016 ). It was shown that high expression of miR-431 prevents synaptic plasticity and memory dysfunction in APP/PS1 mice by reducing the expression of Smad4 (Ge et al. 2023 ). In addition, increasing miR-574-5p expression levels promote neurogenesis but decrease the number of neural progenitor cells, and conversely, decreasing miR-574-5p expression levels inhibits neurogenesis (Zhang et al. 2014 ). Moreover, miR-574-5p might be a potential target downstream of circ_0003391 (Liu et al. 2020a , b ). Low expression of circ_0000950 is regulated through the miR-103/Prostaglandin-endoperoxide synthase 2 (PTGS2) axis, which reduces apoptosis and promotes neuronal growth (Yang et al. 2019 ). Exosomal lnc17A uncouples the Adenosine-5’-triphosphate (ATP) hydrolysis and RNA double dissociation bypass, thereby maintaining long-term neuronal plasticity (Wang et al. 2022 ) (Fig. 1 ) (Table 2 ). Induce phenotypic transformation of microglia cells to slow down the progression of AD In summary, modulating the expression levels of ncRNAs may improve the accumulation of Aβ, excessive phosphorylation of Tau, inflammation, oxidative stress, vascular function, and neuronal regeneration, with the potential to slow down or treat AD. AD is a multifactorial condition, and targeting a single factor alone may not result in effective treatment. With in-depth research on AD, it has been found that microglial cells play a crucial role in both healthy and diseased brains. They are the main resident innate immune cells of the central nervous system, participating in immune responses (Yousefizadeh et al. 2022 ), controlling neuronal signal transmission (Milinkeviciute et al. 2019 ) and play a crucial role in maintaining the stability of neuronal synapses (Sominsky et al. 2018 ) and synaptic plasticity (Milinkeviciute et al. 2019 ; Paolicelli et al. 2011 ). In addition, microglia promote programmed cell death and clearance of apoptotic cells by releasing reactive oxygen species and anti-inflammatory cytokines (Arcuri et al. 2017 ; Sierra et al. 2013 ). They also serve as a key component of amyloid cascade (Yousefizadeh et al. 2022 ), enhancing their phagocytic function and reducing amyloid plaques (Singh et al. 2022 ). And it can drive Tau phosphorylation/aggregation of neurons, leading to the spread of Tau pathology in the brain (Maphis et al. 2015 ). ncRNAs may lead to dysfunction of oligodendrocytes and astrocytes in several neurodegenerative diseases by inhibiting the inhibition of miRNA on target proteins (Chen et al. 2021a , b , c , d ). Research has found that circ-Epc1 regulates the transition from M1 phenotype to M2 phenotype in microglia and the expression of inflammatory cytokines through the interaction with miR-770-3p/Triggering receptor expressed on myeloid cell 2 (TREM2) pathway (Liu et al. 2022a , b ). Circ_0000518 promotes M1 polarization of macrophages/microglial cells via the FUS/CaMKKβ/AMPK pathway (Jiang et al. 2022a , b ). Low-expressing circ_0004381 down-reduced Presenilin 1 (PSEN1) expression through the miR-647/ PSEN1 axis, thereby promoting microglial polarization towards the M2 anti-inflammatory phenotype (Li et al. 2022a , b , c ). Knockdown of lncTUG1 expression can promote microglial polarization to M2 through the miR-145a-5p/NF-κB axis (Wang et al. 2019a , b ). Low expression of lncGAS5 reduces Notch1 expression through the miR-146a-5p/Notch1 axis, thereby promoting M1 to M2 polarisation (Zhang et al. 2019a , b ). MiR-7a-5p, miR-29a-3p, and miR-146a-5p are key participants in controlling the phagocytic ability of microglial cells (Scoyni et al. 2024 ). MiR-146a can induce a phenotypic transition in microglial cells, reduce the production of pro-inflammatory cytokines, and enhance phagocytic function, thus protecting neurons (Liang et al. 2021a , b ), MiR-155 exerts its effects through Toll-like receptor 4 (TLR4)/NF-κB pathway and Nrf2 signaling pathway. Activation of NF-κB promotes M1 polarization of microglia cells and inhibits anti-inflammatory M2 phenotype (He et al. 2023 ; Tufekci et al. 2021 ) (Table 2 ). In addition, two drugs AL002 and AL003 that regulate microglial cells have entered phase I clinical trials and have achieved desirable results respectively ( NCT03635047 ) and ( NCT03822208 ) (Yousefizadeh et al. 2022 ). These data suggest that treatment strategies based on ncRNA-glial cell axis hold promise to become the main approach for improving the therapy of AD in the future. mentioned above, ncRNAs play a crucial role in the development and progression of AD according to studies using cells, animal models, and human patients. These ncRNAs have the ability to regulate key genes involved in AD, indicating their potential as therapeutic targets. Investigating the specific mechanisms by which these ncRNAs contribute to AD pathophysiology could lead to the development of safer and more effective therapeutic approaches for AD. One promising approach is oligonucleotide therapy, which is an emerging tool designed to target ncRNAs. Compared with traditional therapies, ncRNA therapeutic approaches have lower toxicity and the advantage of specific targeting (Liu et al. 2022a , b ). However, the method still has some limitations: ① Many ncRNAs not only have low concentrations in body fluids, but also require complex extraction and isolation methods; ② the easy degradability of ncRNAs makes it difficult to ensure their preservation after administration; ③ all existing ncRNA detection methods have inherent flaws; and ④ high-throughput sequencing is both expensive and wasteful (Liu et al. 2022a , b ). Therefore, further research is needed to optimize these detection methods and develop more accurate diagnostic criteria for clinical use. Furthermore, recent studies have suggested that another type of ncRNA, called PIWIinteracting RNAs (piRNAs), might also be involved in the pathogenesis of AD (Qiu et al. 2017 ). Specifically, piRNAs have shown a significant correlation with Tau levels and the A β42/40 ratio, which are key markers of AD pathology. This finding suggests that analyzing piRNA levels could potentially serve as a diagnostic marker to monitor changes in Tau levels or amyloid pathology in patients with AD (Jain et al. 2019 ). Advantages and challenges of exosome therapy With the progress made in the study of exosomes and the advances in techniques for isolating them, the combination of exosomes with ncRNAs has emerged as a solution to two problems in drug development. Firstly, it addresses the issue of drugs being unable to penetrate the blood-brain barrier (Yu et al. 2020 ), which has long been a challenge in treating neurological disorders such as AD (Bhatt et al. 2021 ; Yang et al. 2021 ). Secondly, it tackles the problem of immune system activation (Iranifar et al. 2019 ). By using exosomal ncRNAs as therapeutic agents, these obstacles can be overcome, providing a promising avenue to develop effective treatments. However, despite the potential of exosomal ncRNAs, there are still significant challenges to their use as therapeutic drugs. One major hurdle lies in effectively monitoring and directing exosomes to their target receptor regions (Wang et al. 2022 ). Exosomes are small vesicles released by cells, making them difficult to track and control once they are administered. Consequently, it remains a daunting task to ensure the precise delivery of exosomal ncRNAs to their intended destinations within the brain (Wang et al. 2022 ). Therefore, there is a need to better explore the expression patterns and the modulation of pathogenesis by exosomal ncRNAs in patients with AD. The ultimate goal and clinical translational challenges of exosome-ncRNA research The ultimate goals of exosome-ncRNAs therapy for AD are closely related to the challenges of clinical translation. Ultimate goals: ① Early diagnosis and intervention: By identifying specific exosome-ncRNAs markers, achieve early diagnosis of AD and intervene before the disease progresses. ② Personalized medicine: Provide personalized treatment plans based on the specific ncRNAs expression profiles of patients. ③ Improved prognosis: Not just alleviating symptoms, but fundamentally altering the pathological process of AD, reducing cognitive decline in AD patients through treatment and enhancing their ability to perform daily activities. Clinical translation challenges: ① Clinical trial design: Designing appropriate clinical trials to evaluate the effectiveness of exosome-ncRNAs therapy for AD is a complex process. It requires determining the right patient population, treatment dosage, timing of treatment, and endpoints for evaluating therapeutic effects. ② Biomarker validation: ncRNAs biomarkers identified in preclinical studies need to be validated in clinical trials to ensure their predictive value and clinical relevance in humans. ③ Personalized therapy: The heterogeneity of AD patients may necessitate individualized treatment plans, which increases the complexity of clinical trials. ④ Efficacy assessment and safety monitoring: Assessing the long-term efficacy and safety of exosome-ncRNAs therapy requires long-term follow-up, which can lead to complexity in data collection and analysis. Moreover, potential side effects and long-term safety of exosome-ncRNAs therapy need to be closely monitored, which may involve complex biomarker testing and clinical assessments. Conclusions In summary, in the progression of AD, miRNA dysregulation has been shown to cause pathological reactions such as abnormal production and metabolism of Aβ, excessive phosphorylation of Tau, neuroinflammation, neuronal damage, oxidative stress and abnormal microglia function, which are considered major neuropathological events. Additionally, studies have revealed that ceRNAs contain specific binding sites for specific miRNAs, acting as miRNAs sponges to inhibit the regulatory function of corresponding miRNAs, thereby causing pathological changes. Many ncRNAs have been proposed as potential diagnostic or therapeutic biomarkers for AD. However, in order for ncRNAs to become promising AD-specific therapeutic targets and diagnostic biomarkers, collaborative research between RNA scientists and neurologists is required to further characterize the expression profiles and functions of these RNA molecules. At the same time, with the advancement of exosome research, its advantages of crossing the blood-brain barrier without activating the immune system can serve as a transport vehicle to address the transportation issues of ncRNAs. In conclusion, we believe that ncRNAs open up a new perspective for the treatment of AD. Perhaps in the near future, high-tech diagnostic and therapeutic methods based on ceRNAs will be developed to improve the quality of life for AD patients.
